# Dawnglass – Days Counter — Privacy Policy

**Version 1.4** · Effective date: 23 November 2026

This policy explains how the Dawnglass – Days Counter app (the "App") handles your
information. The App is made by **Sparkyluv**, an independent developer based in Eluru,
Andhra Pradesh, India ("we", "us").

## 1. The short version
- **We collect no personal data through the App.**
- The App has **no internet access**, no account, no ads, no analytics and no
  tracking. It cannot send anything to us or anyone else.
- Everything you create stays **on your phone**, and you can delete it at any time.
- We do not sell or share personal information.

## 2. What stays on your phone (and never reaches us)

| Data | Why the App keeps it | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Countdown titles, dates and order | To show your countdowns and widgets | App-private storage on your phone |
| Settings (theme, time format, etc.) | To remember your choices | App-private storage |
| Favourite quotes | To show your favourites | App-private storage |
| Your wallpaper photo (if you choose one) | To draw your wallpaper | A private copy on your phone. Location and camera details are **removed** from the copy |
| Daily planner tasks (name, time, days) and your ticks (if you use the planner) | To show your plan, your weekly and monthly reports, and your next task on the wallpaper | App-private storage. When you remove a task, its name is erased; only its past ticks stay for your reports |
| A name for your monthly picture (optional) | To print it on the picture when you share | App-private storage |
| Weekly plan lines (one short line per day, if you use the weekly plan) and whether the plan repeats every week | To show your week in the planner and on the weekly widget | App-private storage |
| When Tikku last said hello (a date, a count and a time) | So the "Idle buddy" appears at most twice a day | App-private storage |
| The lock-screen timer, while one runs: when it ends, whether to keep the screen on and whether to show the dial | To show the time left on your lock screen and say "Time's up" | App-private storage; erased when the timer ends or is stopped |

Other apps cannot read this storage. To delete everything, use
**Settings → Delete all my data**, or uninstall the App.

**What other software on your own phone can see.** We send nothing anywhere, but
Android itself lets a few things you have installed or allowed see what any app shows:
- **Your home-screen app** draws widgets, so it can see what our widgets display.
- **Apps you have given notification access** can read our reminder and planner check-in notifications.
- **Your keyboard app** sees what you type, as it does in every app.
- **Accessibility services and screen recorders** you have switched on can read the screen.

To keep countdown titles, planner task names and weekly plan lines out of widgets, notifications and the wallpaper, turn on
**Settings → Hide titles outside the app**. To blank the App in the recent-apps
screen and block screenshots, turn on **Settings → Hide app in recent apps**.

## 3. Permissions

| Permission | Why |
|---|---|
| Photo Picker (no permission needed) | You choose one photo; the App sees only that photo, not your gallery |
| Run at start-up | So widgets keep counting correctly after your phone restarts |
| Notifications (asked once after setup; you can say no, and turn reminders and check-ins off in Settings at any time) | To remind you when a countdown reaches zero, to ask whether you finished a planned task, and to tell you when your reports are ready. The lock screen hides titles and task names |
| Alarms & reminders (**Settings → On-time updates**, on by default; on newer Android versions Android asks you to allow it once, and you can turn it off any time) | So widgets change exactly at midnight and reminders and check-ins arrive on time. It only sets timers on your phone and sends nothing anywhere |
| Open your clock app (granted automatically, no pop-up) | So tapping the clock on the live wallpaper opens your phone's own clock app (or just its alarm list, if the clock app can't be opened directly). To find it, the App asks Android only which app handles alarms — it cannot see your other apps. The App never sets, reads or changes your alarms |

The App does **not** ask for internet, location, contacts, camera, microphone or
storage permissions.

### Sharing your monthly report
When you tap **Share** on your monthly report, the App makes a picture with your month's
numbers (consistency, tasks done, best streak and stars) and, only if you type one, a name.
**Task names are never on the picture.** The App hands the picture and a link to the App on
Google Play to the app **you** choose (for example a messaging app); the App itself sends
nothing. What happens next is up to that app and its privacy policy. The picture is kept in
the App's private storage until you share again or delete all your data.

## 4. Backups and new phones
The App's data is **excluded from cloud backups**. Android's direct
phone-to-phone transfer may copy your countdowns and settings to your new
phone; this copy goes from device to device and does not pass through us.

## 5. Google's own processing
Google Play and Android are run by Google, which may process data under its own
privacy policy (https://policies.google.com/privacy) — for example when you
download the App, or crash and usage reports that you allow your phone to
share. If you allow that sharing, Google may give us summary or de-identified
crash statistics; we use them only to fix bugs.

## 6. Links to our videos
The quote widget can show a ▶ button that opens **our own playlists on YouTube**
(calming sounds from 10 PM to 6 AM, motivational videos at other times). Tapping it
opens the YouTube app or your browser; the App itself sends nothing to YouTube or to us.
Once you are in YouTube, Google's privacy policy (https://policies.google.com/privacy)
and YouTube's terms apply.

## 7. If you contact us
If you email us, we receive your email address and whatever you write. We use
it only to reply and help you, and delete it within 12 months after the
conversation ends, unless the law requires us to keep it longer.

## 8. Children
The App is for people aged 13 and over; under-18s should use it with a parent's
or guardian's agreement. The App collects no data from anyone, including children.
If you believe a child has sent us personal information by email, contact us and
we will delete it.

## 9. Your rights
Wherever you live — including under India's Digital Personal Data Protection
Act 2023, the EU and UK GDPR, Brazil's LGPD, and US state privacy laws such as
California's — you have rights over personal data that organisations hold about
you, such as access, correction, deletion and complaint.

Because the App gives us no personal data, there is normally nothing for us to
access or delete; the data on your phone is fully under your control. For email
you have sent us, you can ask us to see, correct or delete it (section 11).

You can also complain to a data-protection authority — in India, the **Data
Protection Board of India**; in the EU/UK, your local supervisory authority.

## 10. Changes to this policy
If this policy changes, we'll show the new version in the App before it takes
effect. **If we ever add a feature that collects personal data** (for example
sharing quotes with other users), we will update this policy, tell you
**before** that feature is available, and ask for your consent where the law
requires it. Until then, the App stays fully offline.
